Title: Discontinuation and Reinitiation Strategy for Therapy G in Condition T
1. Background

Condition T is a progressive disorder with variable response to long-term therapy. Evidence suggests that continuous therapy may not be required in all patients.

2. Population

This guideline applies to adult patients aged 18–85 years who have received Therapy G for at least 24 weeks.

3. Intervention

Therapy G is discontinued after 24 weeks in patients who achieve stable clinical response. Reinitiation is considered only if symptoms recur.

4. Monitoring

Monitoring focuses on post-discontinuation relapse detection, not treatment maintenance.

Patients must be assessed at 4 weeks and 8 weeks after discontinuation.
Conditional monitoring rule 1:
If symptoms increase by ≥15%, therapy must be reinitiated within 7 days.

Conditional monitoring rule 2:
If no relapse occurs within 12 weeks, monitoring frequency may be reduced to every 24 weeks.

No monitoring is required during active treatment phases beyond baseline.

5. Outcomes

Successful discontinuation is defined as absence of symptom recurrence within 12 weeks.

Relapse is defined as ≥20% increase in symptom score.

6. Risks

Premature discontinuation increases relapse risk by approximately 25%. Continued therapy increases long-term adverse effects by approximately 10%.

7. Evidence Summary

Evidence is inconclusive. One study shows improved quality of life after discontinuation, while another shows increased relapse rates.
