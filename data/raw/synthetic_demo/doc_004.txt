Title: Adaptive Management of Therapy D Based on Dynamic Biomarker Response in Condition Q
1. Background

Chronic Condition Q is a fluctuating inflammatory disorder affecting approximately 6–9% of adults aged 30–70 years. Disease progression is characterised by episodic increases in inflammatory biomarkers rather than steady decline. Therapy D is used to modulate inflammatory activity in a dynamic manner.

2. Population

This guideline applies to adult patients aged 18–75 years with confirmed Condition Q and at least one documented biomarker fluctuation within the past 6 months. Patients with stable biomarker levels over 12 months are excluded.

3. Intervention

Therapy D is initiated at 100 mg daily. Dose adjustments are not time-based but are triggered by biomarker changes. Dose escalation to 150 mg occurs only when biomarker levels increase by ≥20% from baseline.

4. Monitoring

Monitoring is event-driven rather than fixed-schedule.

Baseline laboratory testing must be performed prior to initiation. Subsequent testing is triggered when symptoms worsen or when patient-reported scores increase by ≥15%.

Patients must perform self-reported symptom tracking every 7 days.

Conditional monitoring rule 1:
If inflammatory biomarkers increase by ≥20%, laboratory testing must occur within 72 hours.

Conditional monitoring rule 2:
If no biomarker increase is observed for 12 weeks, monitoring frequency may be reduced to every 16 weeks.

No fixed periodic monitoring schedule is required.

5. Outcomes

Treatment success is defined as stabilisation of biomarker variability within ±10% over a 12-week period.

Instability is defined as ≥20% fluctuation within any 4-week window.

6. Risks

Common adverse effects include fatigue (18%) and gastrointestinal symptoms (12%). Over-adjustment of therapy increases risk of metabolic imbalance by approximately 10%.

7. Evidence Summary

Evidence is mixed. Two randomised trials suggest benefit from dynamic adjustment strategies, while one observational study reports no significant difference compared to fixed monitoring schedules.
