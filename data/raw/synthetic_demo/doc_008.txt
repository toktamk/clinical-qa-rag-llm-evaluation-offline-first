## **Title: Preventive Use of Therapy H in High-Risk Population U**

### **1. Background**

Condition U is a preventable disorder with an incidence of 2–4% annually in high-risk populations. Therapy H is used as a preventive intervention to reduce incidence rates.

### **2. Population**

This guideline applies to adults aged 30–75 years with a documented high-risk profile defined by ≥2 risk factors. Patients without risk factors are excluded.

### **3. Intervention**

Therapy H is administered at 25 mg daily for a preventive duration of 12 months. Therapy may be extended to 24 months in high-risk individuals.

### **4. Monitoring**

Baseline risk assessment and laboratory testing must be performed prior to initiation. Follow-up evaluations must occur at 1 month and 3 months.

Patients should undergo monitoring every 3 months during therapy. After 12 months, monitoring may be reduced to every 6 months if risk factors remain stable.

**Conditional monitoring rule:**
If a new risk factor emerges during therapy, monitoring frequency must increase to monthly assessments for 3 months.

### **5. Outcomes**

Preventive success is defined as absence of Condition U during the monitoring period. Risk reduction of 30–45% is expected in adherent patients.

### **6. Risks**

Common adverse effects include mild fatigue (10%) and headache (8%). Serious adverse effects occur in less than 1% of patients.

### **7. Evidence Summary**

Evidence is based on three preventive trials involving 2,100 patients. Results show a statistically significant reduction in incidence compared to placebo.
